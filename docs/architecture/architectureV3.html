1 · Data flow

Data flow: from source files to the app on your phone

Data moves left to right. Body drawings and an exercise list go in on the left. Python tools on the PC turn them into data files, which are packed into the app and read when it starts. The only thing the app writes is your own workout log, saved on the phone.

tools run on the PC only

Re-run python tools/appData/buildAppData.py when a body drawing, an exercise share, a target preset or the cardio model changes. If a share or offset is out of range, an id is unknown, or a muscle can't be reached, it stops and writes nothing.

appData generated, ships

Finished data files, read by AppData.loadAll() in app/core/appData.gd. The body PNGs ship exactly as made: each has a .png.import set to "keep", so Godot never re-compresses them.

saves written by the app

The only files the app writes, all through app/core/storage.gd. Small, versioned, crash-safe. Settings → "Export backup" writes them all into one backup file.

2 · App structure

App structure: main parts and how they connect

AppRoot (app/main.gd, the script on app/main.tscn) is the top node of the app. It keeps the stack of open screens and the layer for sheets and toasts. Three services stay loaded the whole time (Godot "autoloads", listed in project.godot): AppData holds the shipped files, Storage your saves, AppTheme the look. Three helpers, HeatEngine, HeatGradients and Targets, only calculate. Screens are built from shared widgets in app/ui/.

Update loop: a screen calls a Storage function such as Storage.setEntrySets(). That ends in save() in storage.gd, which writes the file and emits changed("workouts") (a Godot signal: a broadcast message). Each screen connects to it in its _ready() and refreshes in _onStorageChanged(). Screens never call each other directly.
Look change: AppTheme.apply() builds one Godot theme from the picked style + palette (page 12) and emits themeChanged. _onThemeChanged() in main.gd calls rebuild() on every open screen (a new style: _restyle()).

4 · Saved data

Saved data: the five save files and their fields

Each kind of data is saved in its own JSON file in Godot's user:// folder, all written by app/core/storage.gd. loadAll() reads all five at start-up and save() rewrites one whenever its data changes. Every file is wrapped as {"version": 1, "data": …}, so a future layout change can upgrade old files (_migrate()).

Where user:// is: Android /data/data/com.aphyr.hotmuscles/files/ (private to the app; uninstalling deletes it) · Windows %APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\hotMuscles\ · backups go in user://backups/backup_<date-time>.json plus the clipboard.

5 · Heat calculation

Heat calculation: from exercises to muscle colours

Each exercise in appData/exercises.json lists the regions it works and how much of a set counts for each (its shares). HeatEngine.effectiveSets() in app/core/heatEngine.gd multiplies your sets by the shares and adds them up over the period on show (page 10). HeatGradients.amountColour() turns each total into a colour.

exercise record appData/exercises.json
id · name"Ab_Roller" · Ab Roller
equipment · category12 kinds · 7 kinds
forearmKindnone | grip | direct
curatedyes = hand-tuned shares
targets: one share per region (1.0 = a full set)

Where the shares come from

Hand-tuned (96): typed in tools/appData/curatedExercises.json. Researched (682): researchedExercises.json, each row with a confidence; the 97 low ones show "rough data".

Automatic: only for an exercise no list covers (none today): buildAppData.py splits the database's coarse tags (primary 1.0, secondary 0.5) with its defaultSpread table. Stretching, cardio (149): no heat; cardio counts minutes (page 9).

Grips: 201 exercises have forearmKind "grip": HeatEngine.entryHeat() skips their forearm share when you tick grips. 35 are "direct" and always count.

heat[muscle] = Σ sets × share
periodWorkouts() → effectiveSets() → entryHeat() per entry

Worked example: rear delt (same numbers as the screenshot →)

The face pull works the rear delt fully (1.0); the row and the lat exercises only assist (0.5, 0.3). Tapping a muscle opens the region sheet (openRegion() in weekScreen.gd), which lists these rows from HeatEngine.contributions().

Region sheet for the rear delt

Last 7 days, no fading

workoutsInWindow() keeps the 7 × 24 h before now, not the calendar week. Older workouts drop out. Day, Month, Year: page 10.

Heat number to colour

amountColour() samples t = sets ÷ N on the presets table in heatGradients.gd. The slider sets N; above N is the top colour.

Status vs target band

missedunderokover

targetStatus() checks each region's band in muscles.json (from targetBands in buildAppData.py; 6–12 for most). Under 0.5 sets is missed. rankRegions() sorts the balance list.

30 regions in 7 groups (regions, groups in appData/muscles.json): Back 6 · Shoulders 4 · Chest 3 · Arms 3 · Core 4 · Legs 9 · Neck 1. The picker ranks by HeatEngine.recommend(): share (summed over the picked muscles) + 0.5 × focus, plus small bonuses.

Targets: a weekly goal per muscle, from presets

A target preset is a researched goal for a sport or a look. Per muscle it says how many sets a week to add to, or take off, your baseline (the home slider's N, 12 by default), plus weekly cardio minutes and key exercises. Switch on any mix: Targets in app/core/targets.gd turns them into one target per muscle.

7 · Cardio

Cardio: minutes and effort instead of sets

A cardio exercise is logged as minutes and how hard it felt, never as sets, and gives no muscle heat. Its minutes light one of two "cardio lights" instead: blue for easy cardio, red for hard. The model is researched (tools/appData/cardio.json, sources in data/sources.md) and ships inside appData/targets.json next to the presets.

Logging a cardio row: app/screens/workoutScreen.gd

The row swaps the sets stepper for minutes (changeMinutes(), steps of minuteStep = 5) and adds three effort chips with a talk-test hint under them (setEffort()). Storage.newEntry() starts it at 20 minutes (or 0, by the "new exercises start at" setting) and at the effort you picked last time for that exercise (setEffortMemory()), else its default from cardio.json. Each change saves through Storage.setEntryMinutes() or setEntryEffort().

EffortTalk test (the hint)ZoneLights

Very harda word or two at mostz3red · hard cardio

The efforts are AppData.cardioEfforts / effortById, the lights AppData.cardioLights (each says how much of a minute in each zone lights it). 26 exercises are cardio: 14 from the database, each given a default effort, and 12 added (outdoor run, brisk walk, hike, HIIT circuit…). HeatEngine.isLogged(): an entry counts when it has sets or minutes; 0 of both is planned and dropped on finish.

From logged minutes to the vessels on the body

Month and year average the minutes per week like the sets (scaleHeat() ÷ periodWeeks()). A light with no minutes still shows faintly; a lit one carries a travelling pulse.

Cardio overlay (Targets on too)

The vessel layer: app/components/vesselLayer.gd

BodyView.setVessels() makes a VesselLayer on first use, as a child drawn above the figures, and hands it the levels (setLevels()). The red and blue paths run through each muscle's left / right anchor (AppData.bodyView() anchors), so they follow whichever body and view is shown; the front view gets a beating heart. Under them the shader's dim uniform fades the figure toward dimColour, the card colour. The style picks the finish (glassVessels): Modern draws flat crisp lines, Frutiger glassy glowing tubes (page 12). Colours are a code, not anatomy.

Health minutes = easy + 2 × hard (HeatEngine.healthMinutes(), healthWeights), measured against the WHO guideline: 150 a week is the minimum (healthMinimum), 300 the extra-benefit end (healthExtra). The Balance tab's cardio panel (_fillCardioPanel() in weekScreen.gd) shows both lights as bars against their targets, then this line. Example from the screenshots: 50 easy + 2 × 25 hard = 100 / 150, "Below the WHO minimum".
When the Cardio chip shows: from the first finished workout with any cardio minutes (Targets.hasCardio()). Strength sets never count as cardio, and cardio minutes never heat a muscle.

8 · Home periods and overlays

Home periods and the two one-tap overlays

The home page (app/screens/weekScreen.gd) can count four time spans, and two chips under the body change what it draws: Targets and Cardio. None of this touches the saved workouts; it only changes which workouts are counted and how the numbers are drawn.

Day | Week | Month | Year: weekScreen.gd setPeriod()

PeriodWorkouts countedWhat the body and the tabs show
Daysince local midnight (dayStart())raw sets; Balance lists only the muscles worked today, as part of their weekly target
Weekthe rolling 7 × 24 h (workoutsInWindow())raw sets, every muscle on Balance
Monththe rolling 30 daysaverage sets per week: the total ÷ periodWeeks(), the weeks since your first workout ever (at least 1, at most the whole period)
Yearthe rolling 365 daysthe same average; the Workouts tab has one row per month, its average week on a small body (_monthBuckets())

The ids and days are HeatEngine.periodIds and periodDays. setPeriod() saves the pick (homePeriod), calls refresh() (periodWorkouts() → effectiveSets() → scaleHeat(), also for the cardio minutes) and replays the light-up over exactly 3 s (startReplay(switchReplaySeconds)). The 0–N slider stays the weekly scale in all four, so a month's average reads like a normal week.

Start-up replay: with no workout running, opening the app empties the body and replays the period workout by workout, muscle by muscle from the top down (startReplay(), at most replayMaxSeconds = 4.5 s); a tap on the body skips it.
Week, overlays off

The overlay chips: BodyCard.addFooterChip() → weekScreen.gd _applyOverlays()

Targets setTargetsOverlay()

Shown while a target preset is on (page 8). displayHeat() → HeatEngine.targetHeat() redraws each muscle as its share of its target on the 0–N scale: at target = N, the hot end; a muscle with no target stays plain. The card title becomes "Sets vs targets" and the hint counts the muscles on target (HeatEngine.onTarget()).

The legend: setScaleMode("target") sets HeatRangeSlider.scaleMode, which numbers the slider 0 / 50% / target / 2× instead of sets. Balance lists only muscles with a target, each band running from the target to 2× it (_bandOf()); the region sheet shows "your target".

Cardio setCardioOverlay()

Shown once any cardio was logged (page 9). cardioLevels() → bodyCard.setVessels() → BodyView.setVessels(): red and blue vessels over the body, each as bright as its minutes are of the weekly target. The hint reads "Blue = easy x/y min · Red = hard x/y min" (cardHintText()) and the Balance tab gets the cardio panel with the health line.

Both chips

Remembered in settings.json (overlayTargets, overlayCardio, written once by _writeOverlay()), and both can be on together. _applyOverlays() does everything a chip changes in one pass: the chips' own state, the card title and hint, setScaleMode(), setHeat(displayHeat(shownHeat)), setVessels() and the Balance tab (_fillBalance()). The Balance tab always starts with a targets line: which presets are on, and a button to the Targets screen.

9 · Body rendering

Body rendering: region map texture, palette and shader

Each body is drawn as one textured rectangle by app/components/bodyView.gd. The texture is not a picture. It is a region map, appData/bodyMaps/<body>_<view>.png, baked by tools/appData/bakeBodyMaps.py: each colour channel stores a different number per pixel (like an ID pass in compositing). The shader app/components/bodyMap.gdshader reads those numbers and colours the body on every frame. Below: male_front.png split into its channels.

From a logged workout to colours on the Week screen

1 · Save
Storage.save() writes user://workouts.json and emits changed("workouts").
2 · Count
_onStorageChanged() in weekScreen.gd → refresh() → HeatEngine.effectiveSets(): one number per region.
3 · Colour
bodyCard.setHeat() → bodyView.setHeat(); each number goes through amountColour() into the palette.
4 · Draw
bodyMap.gdshader looks up every pixel's region id in the palette, so the GPU recolours the body.
Why it works this way: an earlier version drew tens of thousands of outline points per body on every frame. Now the GPU gets one rectangle and two small textures, and the phone builds no geometry at all. If a body map file is missing, AppData.bodyMap() logs an error naming the file instead of quietly drawing something else.

10 · Looks

Looks: a style and a palette, picked separately

Settings → Look has two separate picks: a style (shapes, panels, fonts, the feel) and a palette (colours only). Each is one script file in app/looks/. AppTheme (app/core/appTheme.gd) puts the pair together into one Godot theme. A style never changes what a screen does, only how it looks.

The registry: app/looks/looks.gd

stylePaths and palettePaths list the files in menu order; a file's name is its id (idOf()). loadStyles() / loadPalettes() load them, skipping a broken one with an error in the log. defaultStyle "modern" and defaultPalette "ember" are worn before you pick. To add a palette: copy a file, change its name and colours, add its path.

Styles: appStyle.gd and styles/

app/looks/appStyle.gd (class AppStyle) is the base and on its own is the Modern look; styles/modern.gd only names it. A style overrides only what it changes: shape numbers (radii, paddings, text sizes), adjustColours(), box(role, …) for every panel, button and chip, fonts, decorateFrame(), drawCardChrome(), glassVessels.

Frutiger (styles/frutiger.gd): glossy pills, glass cards, a sky page with bubbles and swooshes, glassy vessels, all drawn in code, with app/looks/shapeBox.gd for shapes StyleBoxFlat can't draw.

A saved palette or style that no longer exists falls back to Ember / Modern (_paletteIdOr(), _styleIdOr()).

11 · Behind the scenes

Behind the scenes: building the data and keeping it fast

Two jobs happen out of sight. Scripts in tools/ build the data files once on the PC, so the phone has almost nothing to compute at start-up. And every build is checked against two time limits, so the app stays quick to open and smooth to use.

< 500 ms
main scene → first frame, printed by _onFirstFrame() in main.gd, checked by tests/testStartup.gd and checkBuild.py (fresh-PC first launch: under 2 s)
< 40 ms
slowest frame in any everyday step (tap, scroll, open a screen): worstFrameLimitMs in testStartup.gd. Aim 33 ms (30 fps); v0.3 worst was 27–32 ms

How the app meets those limits

✓

Long lists fill in over several frames. pickerScreen.gd builds firstRows (6), then rowsPerFrame (3) per frame up to pageSize (40); all at once was a 90 ms stutter. weekScreen.gd (firstBalanceRows) and targetsScreen.gd do the same.

✓

Update rows, don't rebuild them. Existing rows are changed or moved in place.

✓

One write for many values. A write costs about 4 ms, so Storage.addEntries() and setProfileValues() save many values at once.

✓

Body maps load in the background. _startMapDecodes() in appData.gd decodes the PNGs on WorkerThreadPool threads.

✓

Idle bodies sleep. bodyView.gd calls set_process(false) once nothing on the body is animating.

Automatic checks in tests/, all run through runGodot.py: testHeatEngine.gd (maths), testStorage.gd (saves, backups), testFlows.gd (every screen with real numbers), testTouchUi.gd (synthetic finger), testKineticScroll.gd, testStartup.gd (timing), testBuildAppData.py (bad data refused). checkBuild.py also opens each exe three times on a throwaway save folder.

Rebuild and ship

python tools/appData/buildAppData.py
python tools/build/buildAll.py
python tools/build/checkBuild.py <exe> <apk>

Builds land in builds/hotMuscles_vNNN (not in the repo; shared on the Releases page). Every build is a new version and builds/ keeps every apk ever made. checkBuild.py fails any build that holds data/, models/, tools/, tests/, docs/ or icons/, or lacks appData/targets.json.
